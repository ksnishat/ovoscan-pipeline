HATCHERY EGG QUALITY CONTROL MANUAL
Internal Reference Document - Quality Assurance Division
Revision 4.2

================================================================================
SECTION 1: SCOPE AND PURPOSE
================================================================================

This manual defines the visual quality criteria and disposition rules for
table and hatching eggs entering the incubation stream. It is the reference
document used by the automated optical inspection system (OvoScan) and by
human graders performing manual verification.

The objective of this procedure is to remove eggs that cannot produce a viable
chick before they consume incubator capacity. Incubator space is the binding
constraint in hatchery economics: an infertile or cracked egg occupies a
tray position for 18 days and returns nothing.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: CLASSIFICATION CATEGORIES
================================================================================

The inspection system assigns each egg to exactly one of two classes.

2.1 FERTILE
    An egg whose internal structure is consistent with a successfully
    fertilised ovum. Visual indicators include:
      - A clearly defined, centrally located blastoderm disc.
      - Uniform yolk shadow without irregular dark mottling.
      - Intact shell with no visible fracture lines under transmitted light.
      - Absence of blood rings, which indicate an embryo that died early.

    Disposition: ACCEPT. Route to the incubation stream.

2.2 INFERTILE
    An egg that shows no evidence of fertilisation, or that shows evidence of
    early embryonic death. Visual indicators include:
      - Absence of a visible blastoderm disc, or a disc with irregular,
        granular appearance.
      - A distinct blood ring encircling the yolk, indicating early mortality.
      - Cloudy or opaque albumen inconsistent with a fresh fertile egg.
      - Shell surface anomalies that correlate with poor hatchability.

    Disposition: REJECT. Remove from the incubation stream.

================================================================================
SECTION 3: SHELL INTEGRITY CRITERIA
================================================================================

Shell defects are the second most common cause of rejection after
infertility. The following defects are grounds for immediate rejection
regardless of internal classification.

3.1 CRACKED SHELL
    Any visible fracture line, including hairline cracks that are only
    detectable under transmitted light. A cracked shell allows bacterial
    ingress and will contaminate adjacent eggs in the tray.

3.2 STAR FRACTURE
    A localised impact site with radiating fracture lines. Typically caused by
    rough handling during collection or transport. Always reject.

3.3 PINHOLE
    A small puncture, often from a broken eggshell fragment in the collection
    belt. Difficult to detect visually; requires transmitted light inspection.

3.4 SHELL-LESS OR SOFT-SHELLED
    An egg with an incomplete or absent calcified shell. Indicates a calcium
    metabolism deficiency in the laying flock. Reject and flag the source
    flock for nutritional review.

3.5 DIRTY SHELL
    Visible faecal or organic contamination covering more than approximately
    10 percent of the shell surface. Reject. Contamination spreads through
    the incubator and reduces hatch rate for the entire tray.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: SIZE AND SHAPE CRITERIA
================================================================================

4.1 UNDERSIZED EGGS
    Eggs below the minimum weight threshold for the breed line. These produce
    undersized chicks with reduced viability. Reject.

4.2 OVERSIZED EGGS
    Eggs above the maximum weight threshold. These are frequently double-yolked
    and will not hatch. Reject.

4.3 ABNORMAL SHAPE
    Markedly elongated, spherical, or ridged eggs. Shape anomalies correlate
    with internal structural anomalies. Reject.

================================================================================
SECTION 5: DISPOSITION RULES
================================================================================

The following decision table is authoritative. When the automated system and a
human grader disagree, the human grader's decision prevails and the case is
logged for model review.

  Classification    Shell Integrity    Disposition
  ----------------  -----------------  --------------------------------------
  FERTILE           Intact             ACCEPT
  FERTILE           Any defect         REJECT (shell defect overrides)
  INFERTILE         Intact             REJECT (infertility)
  INFERTILE         Any defect         REJECT (both grounds)
  UNCERTAIN         Intact             HOLD for manual grading
  UNCERTAIN         Any defect         REJECT

================================================================================
SECTION 6: REQUIRED ACTIONS BY DEFECT CLASS
================================================================================

6.1 INFERTILE - ACTION
    Remove the egg from the incubation stream immediately. Record the source
    flock identifier and collection date. If the infertility rate for a single
    flock exceeds 8 percent over a rolling 7-day window, escalate to the
    Flock Health team for a fertility investigation. Common causes are
    incorrect male-to-female ratio, male infertility, or excessive flock age.

6.2 CRACKED SHELL - ACTION
    Remove the egg immediately. Inspect the upstream collection belt and
    transport trays for the mechanical cause. If cracks exceed 2 percent of
    daily volume, schedule a maintenance review of the collection line.

6.3 DIRTY SHELL - ACTION
    Remove the egg immediately. Review nest box hygiene and litter condition.
    Persistent contamination indicates a housing management issue rather than
    an egg-level issue.

6.4 SOFT-SHELLED - ACTION
    Remove the egg immediately. Flag the source flock for a calcium and
    vitamin D3 nutrition review. Collect a shell quality sample for the
    nutritionist.

6.5 ABNORMAL SHAPE - ACTION
    Remove the egg. Log the anomaly. Isolated occurrences require no further
    action; a cluster indicates a flock-level issue.

================================================================================
SECTION 7: ESCALATION THRESHOLDS
================================================================================

The following thresholds trigger an escalation to the Quality Manager. These
are the values the monitoring system should alert on.

  Metric                              Threshold        Escalation
  ----------------------------------  ---------------  ----------------------
  Infertility rate, rolling 7 days    > 8 percent      Flock Health team
  Crack rate, daily                   > 2 percent      Maintenance review
  Contamination rate, daily           > 1 percent      Housing management
  Soft-shell rate, rolling 7 days     > 0.5 percent    Nutritionist
  Model confidence, rolling 100 eggs  < 0.75 mean      Model review

The model confidence threshold is a system-health signal, not an egg-quality
signal. A sustained drop in mean confidence usually indicates that the
incoming egg population has drifted away from the training distribution, for
example a new breed line or a change in lighting conditions at the inspection
station.

================================================================================
SECTION 8: INSPECTION ENVIRONMENT REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

8.1 LIGHTING
    Transmitted light inspection requires a diffuse backlight at approximately
    2000 to 3000 lux at the egg surface. Direct point sources create glare that
    the optical system may interpret as a shell defect.

8.2 CONVEYOR SPEED
    The inspection station is rated for a maximum conveyor speed of 12 eggs per
    second. Above this rate, motion blur reduces classification accuracy.

8.3 CAMERA CALIBRATION
    Recalibrate the inspection camera at the start of every shift using the
    reference target. Log the calibration result. An uncalibrated camera is the
    most common cause of a sudden accuracy drop.

8.4 AMBIENT TEMPERATURE
    Maintain the inspection area between 15 and 25 degrees Celsius. Condensation
    on the camera lens at low temperatures produces false shell-defect
    detections.

================================================================================
SECTION 9: RECORD KEEPING
================================================================================

Every inspection decision must be logged with the following fields:
  - Timestamp
  - Egg identifier or tray position
  - Assigned classification and confidence score
  - Shell integrity result
  - Final disposition
  - Operator identifier, if manually overridden

Records are retained for 24 months to support hatchability analysis and
customer quality audits.
